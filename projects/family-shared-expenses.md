# 🏡 Family Shared Expenses Plan

**Status:** Draft
**Last updated:** June 14, 2026

---

## The Goal

Create a single central account structure where **all community/household/family expenses** flow through one shared credit card. Anything charged to this card is a **mutual responsibility** — not Brian's or Barbara's individually. It's a family expense, paid from family funds.

---

## Guiding Principles

1. **Shared is shared.** If it goes on the family card, it's a joint obligation — no splitting, no "whose turn," no tracking who paid last.
2. **Personal stays personal.** Individual discretionary spending stays on individual cards.
3. **Simple, not perfect.** The goal is less friction, not perfect categorization. Accept rough consensus over granular tracking.
4. **Transparent by default.** Both Brian and Barbara see the statement monthly. No surprises.
5. **Automatic where possible.** Recurring family bills (utilities, subscriptions, kid stuff) auto-charge to the family card.

## Account & Card Structure

### Option A (Recommended): Joint Credit Card + Joint Checking

```
Joint Checking Account
├── Both incomes deposit here (or fixed transfer from individual accounts)
├── Auto-pay for the family credit card
└── Both have debit cards for cash needs

Joint Credit Card
├── Primary cardholder: whoever has better credit / rewards
├── Authorized user: the other
├── All family expenses charged here
├── Rewards (cash back / points) → family fund
└── Auto-paid in full from joint checking each month
```

### Option B: One Card, Split Deposit

```
Keep individual checking accounts
├── Each sets up automatic transfer to a joint account
│   ├── Brian: $X per paycheck
│   └── Barbara: $Y per paycheck
│
Joint Account
└── Auto-pay the family card
```

### Option C: Add Authorized User on an Existing Card

```
Simplest start:
├── Add Barbara as AU on Brian's best rewards card (or vice versa)
├── Both use that card for family expenses
├── Track family charges separately (easier if used only for family)
└── Pay from whichever account you both agree on
```

## The "Family Fund" — How the Bill Gets Paid

### Monthly Flow

```
1. Family charges accumulate on the shared card
2. Statement comes due (typically 25-day grace period)
3. Payment drawn from the family checking account
4. Family checking funded by both incomes
```

### Funding Method: Proportional to Income (Primary)

Because Brian and Barbara earn meaningfully different amounts, **proportional-to-income contributions** are the primary funding method. Each contributes the same *share of their take-home pay* rather than the same *dollar amount*.

| Income Source | Gross | Net (est.) | Share of Net | Monthly Contribution* |
|--------------|-------|-----------|--------------|----------------------|
| Brian (Tavus) | ~$25,000/mo | ~$17K | ~45% | ~$18K |
| Barbara (Salesforce) | ~$30K–$33K/mo | ~$21K | ~55% | ~$22K |
| **Total household** | | **~$38K/mo net** | **100%** | **~$40K/mo** |

\* Contribution sized to cover spend + buffer + carrying costs — see buffer calc below.

The card gets paid in full every month — no carrying a balance. Any excess in the joint account builds a family reserve.

### Alternative: 50/50 Flat Contribution

If proportional accounting feels like overhead, **50/50 flat** is the simpler fallback — each contributes the same dollar amount regardless of income. Easier to administer, but the lower earner feels a bigger relative pinch. Use this only if both are comfortable with the asymmetry.

### Sizing the Buffer

Monthly outflows from the joint account aren't just household spend — Category D (investment property) plus mortgage/loan service (see below) also draw from it. Realistic monthly draw:

| Bucket | Monthly |
|--------|---------|
| Household + kids + subscriptions (A, B, C) | $8K–$12K |
| Construction-phase carrying costs (Austin + SF HELOC + Sonoma land — see [financing.md](../properties/financing.md)) | $7K–$8K |
| **Total monthly draw** | **$15K–$20K** |

A one-month buffer isn't enough when construction draws can spike unpredictably. **Recommended reserve: $18K–$20K from each** (~$36K–$40K parked in the joint account) — roughly two months of total outflow. Rebalance quarterly: if balance trends up, contribute less; if it trends down, contribute more.

## Tax Hygiene: Keep Investment Property Spend Separate

> ⚠️ **Mixing personal and investment property expenses on the same card complicates Schedule E.**

Category D (Austin duplex, Sonoma lot, construction-related) generates **deductible expenses** on Schedule E for the rental/investment side. If those charges live on the same joint personal card as groceries and kid stuff, the bookkeeper (or you, in April) has to line-item-categorize every statement to separate deductible from non-deductible — error-prone and audit-unfriendly.

**Recommended:**

1. **Open a separate account/card dedicated to investment-property spend** — either a second business credit card under a property-holding entity (LLC) or, at minimum, a separate personal card used *only* for property expenses.
2. **Move Category D off the family card.** Austin maintenance, Sonoma taxes, construction-related shared costs all flow through the property card.
3. **Pay the property card from a separate property account** funded from rental income + owner contributions, not from the family joint checking.
4. **If an LLC owns the property**, the card should be in the LLC's name — this preserves the corporate veil and makes year-end accounting trivial (one statement = one expense category for the entity).

This is mostly a one-time setup cost (open one account, redirect ~5–10 vendor charges) that pays back every tax season.

> Talk to the CPA before implementing — they may have a preferred structure (especially if there's already an LLC for either property).

## Gotchas & Guardrails

### What If…
| Scenario | Plan |
|----------|------|
| One person overspends on family card | It's a shared card — both see charges. The rule is: if in doubt, ask. Large purchases ($500+) should get a quick heads-up. |
| Disagreement on what's "family" | Default to "if it benefits the household or kids, it's family." If still unclear, leave it off the card and pay personally. |
| Emergency / unexpected expense | Put it on the family card. That's what the buffer is for. Discuss after. |
| Rewards / points | All rewards from the family card → family fund (vacations, upgrades). Not personal. |
| One person's income changes | Adjust contributions at the quarterly review. |

### Recommended Guardrails
- **No balance carried.** Pay in full every month. This card is a convenience, not a loan.
- **$500+ requires a text.** Quick heads-up before big charges.
- **Monthly 10-minute review.** Scan the statement together. Catch surprises early.
- **Annual true-up.** In January, reconcile contributions vs. actual spending and adjust.
